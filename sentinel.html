THE PROBLEM

A model rarely fails loudly. It gets a little worse every week.

Customer behaviour changes, a data source is updated, fraudsters adapt. The model keeps returning scores, and nothing looks broken. The first sign is usually a loss, a complaint or an auditor's question. By then the decay has been running for months.

WHAT PAYESH WATCHES

Four signals, checked on every period of production data.

Each one answers a question a risk owner actually has to answer.

Score and feature drift

Has the shape of the model's scores, or of its input features, moved away from the reference period? Measured with the Population Stability Index (PSI) and ranked, so you see which feature moved first.

Recall decay

Of the cases that really were fraud (or default, or churn), how many is the model still catching compared with launch? Shown period by period when you have confirmed outcomes.

Decision threshold

Sometimes the model is fine and the cut-off is stale. PAYESH weighs the cost of a miss against the cost of a false alarm and suggests a threshold, which can recover performance without retraining.

Evidence trail

Every alert, threshold change and decision is recorded in a tamper-evident log, so you can show a regulator or auditor what was known, and when.

HOW IT WORKS

From a reference baseline to a recommendation you can act on.

1

Set the reference

PAYESH takes a period when the model was known to be healthy and records what its scores and features looked like.

2

Compare every new period

Production data is split into periods and compared with the reference, for scores, for each feature and, where outcomes are known, for recall.

3

Alert at the level you choose

You set how sensitive the monitor is. Small shifts go on a watch list, and larger ones raise an alert with the feature that caused it.

4

Recommend and record

Each alert comes with a plain-language next step, such as investigate a feature, adjust a threshold or schedule a retrain. It is logged in the evidence trail.
